A book written online, in the open. Chapman invents a word — *meaningness* — for the
quality of being meaningful and/or
meaningless, and then refuses both of the obvious answers. Eternalism fixates on
meaning. Nihilism denies it. Both are wrong, and both make you miserable.

The alternative he calls the complete stance: meaningness is always both nebular and
patterned, and you can work with that rather than against it. Four steps, applied to
ethics, purpose, identity, and society. Buddhist in its roots — nebulosity is close
to emptiness, pattern close to form — but written for people who have already
rejected religious answers.
