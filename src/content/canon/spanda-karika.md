Fifty-two verses, read as a running commentary on the Śiva Sūtras. Vasugupta found
the Sūtras; his disciple Kallaṭa brought these stanzas to light. They carry one idea
further than the Sūtras do: that the absolute is not cold intellection but *spanda* —
a throb, a creative pulsation, something that moves without ever leaving itself.

Four classical commentaries survive, Kṣemarāja's among the most authoritative. The
verses work through the nature of spanda, the arising of natural knowledge, and the
powers that follow from it.

**Read with:** Jaideva Singh's translation (*Spanda-Kārikās: The Divine Creative
Pulsation*), or Dyczkowski's *The Stanzas on Vibration*, which carries four
commentaries at once.
