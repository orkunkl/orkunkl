A near enemy is a quality that so closely resembles a virtue that it is easily
mistaken for one, while being its corruption. The near enemy of equanimity is
indifference. The near enemy of compassion is pity.

Wallis takes the slogans everyone repeats — *you create your own reality*, *be your
best self*, *follow your bliss*, *everything happens for the best* — and shows how
each one works: it offers relief from something intolerable, and quietly forecloses
the inquiry that would actually produce wisdom. Each chapter ends by pointing at the
deeper truth the slogan was flattening.
