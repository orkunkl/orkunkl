Project Liminality is David Rug's attempt to build the missing layer between two things
everyone agrees are broken: how we think together, and how we coordinate. He studied
physics in Heidelberg, ran into the GameB conversation, and came away with what he
calls a *physics of communication* — the claim that sense-making and coordination are
not soft problems but have structure you can design against.

The channel's central work is **Immanent Metaphysics**, the formal system Forrest
Landry developed through the 1990s. The video I came in through is
[**The Age of Miracles**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yrgSwgqclJU), which carries
the whole argument in one arc — from Landry's system, down through Moloch, and back out
to the more beautiful world. It is the piece I would hand someone first.

Its compact statement is *love is that which
enables choice; fear is what consumes it*. From that single distinction Rug derives two
modes of mind: **domesticated**, which uses story as a tool — narrative — and
**liminal**, which serves story as a being — vision. Domesticated mind cannot feel that
its choices are entangled with everyone else's, so the behaviour of others looks like
chance to it. From there the step to "winners and losers, predator and prey" is small,
and from there to game theory is automatic. The observation I keep returning to is
this: Nash's framework is not wrong, it is *local*. Rug calls it the physics of fear.
It describes exactly the slice of the world where fear has already consumed choice.

This is Moloch's territory, arrived at from the inside. The multipolar trap is not a
failure of intelligence — it is what intelligence looks like once the sense of
connection is gone.

Then the **third attractor**, which is where the series lands. The frame comes from
Daniel Schmachtenberger's *In Search of the Third Attractor*: if the first attractor is
civilisational breakdown and the second is the control dystopia built in response, the
third is neither. Project Liminality's own pass at it is in
[**The InterBrain**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5dBRmjF8mc), which opens on
exactly that chapter. What it adds is specificity. Stewarded technology,
sense-making worth the name, and a coordination layer that makes the connection between
people *visible* rather than merely asserted. That last part is the software the video
is named for: a knowledge-gardening system that organises along your actual
relationships instead of hierarchies — Dream Nodes implemented as git repositories, a
Coherence Beacon that surfaces links you did not know you had.

Two things stay with me. First, that the useful diagnosis of Moloch is perceptual
rather than economic — no protocol fixes a trap whose root is that people cannot feel
each other. Second, that it is possible to hold an extremely abstract frame and still
ship software. That combination is rarer than either half, and worth more.
