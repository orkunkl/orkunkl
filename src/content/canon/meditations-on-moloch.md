Alexander opens with Ginsberg's Moloch section from *Howl* and then asks the question
underneath it: if everyone hates the system, who keeps it running? His answer is not
an ancient demon. It's a structure with no one at the wheel — a multipolar trap.

The thought experiment that does the work: a country where every citizen must shock
themselves eight hours a day, and anyone who refuses is killed by everyone else. Every
single person hates it. Nobody can stop. There is no authority to appeal to, because
the thing that enforces the rule is the rule.

Then a run of real examples — the prisoner's dilemma, dollar auctions, a lake of fish
farms that each quietly disconnects its filter and poisons the water for everyone
including itself, the Malthusian trap. All of them the same shape: from a god's-eye
view there is an obviously better outcome; from inside, no individual can get there
without great risk to themselves. Bostrom's phrase for it is a dictatorless dystopia.

This is the essay I keep returning to because it is the problem I work on. A protocol
is what you build when "everyone just stop" is not a mechanism — when you need the
coordination itself to be the thing that holds, rather than everyone's continued
goodwill.
